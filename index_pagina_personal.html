The Full Data Stack
Ingestion, warehouse, transformation, BI. 9 sources. 7 domains. Built from zero.
Example — Architecture decision
Evaluated and selected Hevo (ingestion), Snowflake (warehouse), dbt (transformation), Sigma (BI) — negotiated all contracts. Designed a 3-layer medallion: RAW (23 schemas) → STAGING → PRODUCTION → SELF_SERVICE. Stack went live within 6 months.
-- 3-layer medallion in dbt_project.yml models: finalis_wh: FINALIS_RAW: # Bronze — raw ingestion PORTAL: {+materialized: incremental} FINALIS_STAGING: # Silver — cleaned & joined MASTER: {+materialized: table} FINALIS_DB: # Gold — governed metrics SELF_SERVICE: {+materialized: view}

02
Governed Semantic Layer
~50 KPIs in version-controlled SQL. One definition, one owner, one variance threshold per metric.
Example — MRR movement model
Finance, Sales, and CS all had different ARR numbers. I wrote a single SQL definition per metric. The MRR model classifies each customer's monthly change into Net New, Expansion, Downgrade, or Churn — and injects a synthetic next-month zero row to catch delayed churn signals before they show in renewals.
-- SaaS MRR movement per customer per month CASE WHEN COALESCE(past_amount,0) = 0 AND current_amount > 0 THEN current_amount ELSE 0 END AS Net_New_MRR, -- Annualised bookings from new + expansion (Net_New_MRR + Expansion_MRR) * 12 AS actuals_bookings

-- Cortex generates this from plain English: -- "Top firms by revenue last 6 months" SELECT firm_name, SUM(deal_gross_fee) AS total_fees, COUNT(deal_id) AS deals_closed FROM SELF_SERVICE.DEALS WHERE closing_date >= DATEADD('month',-6,CURRENT_DATE) AND deal_status = 'WON' GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 2 DESC LIMIT 10

Problem 1 — Three teams, three ARR numbers. Nobody trusted the data.
Metric Governance+
The situation: Finance calculated ARR from Stripe. Sales from Salesforce opportunity amounts. CS from a manual spreadsheet. All three different every week. Every business review opened with a 20-minute argument about the number before anyone discussed strategy.
What I did: Traced all three calculations to their source systems. Found the discrepancies: Finance used billing date, Sales used close date, CS included non-recurring fees. Wrote a single SQL definition — sourced from Stripe, adjusted for deferrals, filtered to active recurring plans. Added a named owner, a ±2% variance threshold, and a weekly audit that flagged deviations automatically.
-- Official ARR — version-controlled in dbt -- Owner: Finance · Threshold: ±2% week-over-week SELECT customer_id, firm_name, mrr * 12 AS arr, COALESCE(arr_yr_1, arr_yr2) AS arr_last_year, commissions_rate, plan, renewal_date FROM finance.parameter_subscription WHERE month IS NOT NULL AND __hevo__marked_deleted = FALSE
✓ Result: Metric disputes stopped. The CEO dashboard launched with a single ARR figure. Four teams stopped producing competing versions within the first week.
Problem 2 — Customer Success finding out about churn after it happened.
Churn Intelligence+
The situation: CS reps only knew a client was at risk when the renewal conversation failed. Login frequency had dropped months earlier, deal activity had slowed, support tickets had increased. Nobody was watching those signals because they lived in five different systems.
What I did: Built a unified customer-360 model pulling signals from five sources: platform login frequency (Hub), deal activity (Airtable), form completion rates (Portal), support escalations (Intercom), billing status (Stripe). Weighted signals into a churn score per firm, updated weekly. CS received a ranked Monday alert list. High-risk firms got a proactive call before the renewal date.
Signals used: Days since last login · Active deals count · Form completion rate · Open escalations · Overdue invoices · DAU/MAU trend (last 30 vs prior 30 days)
✓ Result: GRR improved from 93.1% to 98.2% within two quarters. CS shifted from reactive renewal calls to proactive account health management.
Problem 3 — 80 people needed data daily. None of them could write SQL.
AI Self-Service+
The situation: Capital Markets managers, Sales Ops, and CS reps needed data every day. None could query Snowflake. Every request went through the data team via Slack. The team was spending 40%+ of their time on repetitive ad-hoc questions instead of building.
What I did: Deployed Snowflake Cortex Analyst on top of the governed SELF_SERVICE schema. Users type questions in plain English. Cortex generates SQL, runs it, returns results. The key: because the semantic layer was already clean and governed, the AI answers were accurate from day one. No hallucinations about metric definitions — those were enforced at the model level before the AI ever touched them.
Example questions users now answer themselves: "Which firms have the most active deals this month?"  ·  "Show bankers with no deal activity in 60 days"  ·  "What's our GRR trend last 6 quarters?"  ·  "Which industries have the highest average deal size?"
✓ Result: 80+ non-technical users onboarded to self-service analytics. Manual data requests dropped 80%+. The team redirected that time to building new models.
